About

A scientific journey shaped by curiosity and connection.

I am a cell and molecular biologist whose research has developed across cellular stress, ageing and senescence, extracellular-vesicle biology, intercellular communication, proteomics and quantitative mammalian cell-based research. I am increasingly interested in translating this scientific foundation towards biomedical R&D and future technologies for therapeutics, diagnostics and precision healthcare.

My scientific path has taken me from India to Ireland, France and Germany, progressively connecting questions of cellular stress, ageing, disease biology and communication between cells.

Rather than developing around a single technique or experimental system, my research has evolved around a broader biological question: how changes in cellular state influence function, molecular communication and the behaviour of neighbouring cells.

This has led me through complementary research environments spanning redox and mitochondrial biology, translational delivery research, endothelial ageing and senescence, extracellular-vesicle biology, quantitative proteomics and functional cell-based assays.

I am particularly interested in how mechanistic biological understanding can ultimately be connected with technology development and translational biomedical applications.

Current Status

Based in Paris, France. My fixed-term postdoctoral appointment at Université Paris Cité concluded in March 2026. I am currently open to new research, scientific collaboration and translational R&D opportunities.

Foundations

From India to Ireland

My scientific training began in India and developed into an international biomedical research career.

I completed my undergraduate life-science training at the University of Delhi before moving to Ireland for postgraduate study at the University of Galway.

During my MSc in Neuropharmacology, I became increasingly interested in cellular and molecular mechanisms of disease, particularly mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress and autophagy-related biology.

I subsequently worked in Galway on peroxiredoxins, redox signalling and mitochondrial biology. This established an early conceptual foundation for my later research: understanding how cellular stress alters molecular and functional behaviour.

Translational Research

Discovering extracellular vesicles

Translational research introduced me to extracellular vesicles as both biological communicators and potential delivery systems.

Through industry-linked research involving OmniSpirant and the University of Galway, I gained experience with engineered cellular systems and extracellular-vesicle-based therapeutic research.

This experience broadened my perspective from studying molecular stress mechanisms within individual cells towards considering how biological material can move between cells and influence recipient-cell behaviour.

It also strengthened my interest in research that connects fundamental cell biology with the development of new therapeutic approaches and biomedical technologies.

France & Germany

Ageing, senescence & communication

My PhD brought together cellular ageing, vascular biology and extracellular-vesicle-mediated intercellular communication.

I completed my doctoral research through an international cotutelle between Aix-Marseille Université and Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz within the Marie Skłodowska-Curie TiCardio Innovative Training Network.

My work investigated how ageing and cellular senescence alter endothelial cells and the extracellular vesicles they release.

The research combined primary human endothelial-cell systems, extracellular-vesicle isolation and characterisation, cellular phenotyping, recipient-cell experiments, functional assays and quantitative molecular analysis.

International research mobility within the programme also expanded my experience into quantitative proteomics and collaborative research across institutions in France and Germany.

Paris

Quantitative functional biology

My subsequent postdoctoral research extended my work towards quantitative evaluation of functional intracellular delivery.

During my fixed-term appointment in the Lavieu Lab at Université Paris Cité, I worked with quantitative mammalian cell-based systems for evaluating functional cargo delivery.

My contribution focused on optimisation and standardisation of quantitative functional assays, experimental controls and reproducible workflows.

This strengthened an increasingly important part of my scientific approach: moving beyond whether a biological interaction occurs towards quantitatively determining whether it produces a measurable functional consequence.

Scientific Identity

Connecting cellular states with function.

My research interests converge around cellular ageing, stress, intercellular communication and quantitative functional biology.

Extracellular vesicles remain an important experimental and conceptual part of my background, but I see them within a broader biological framework involving cellular-state transitions, molecular communication and functional responses.

I am particularly interested in environments where mechanistic cell biology, quantitative experimentation and emerging technologies can be brought together around translational biomedical questions.

Future Direction

From biological understanding to future technologies

Looking forward, I want to contribute to research and development that translates biological understanding into technologies with practical relevance for human health.

I am particularly interested in R&D at the interface of cell and molecular biology, quantitative experimentation and technology development.

In the longer term, I would like to contribute to the development of future biomedical technologies across areas such as therapeutics, diagnostics, precision medicine and personalised healthcare.

This does not mean limiting my future work to any single disease area or experimental platform. I am interested in scientific environments where strong mechanistic biology can be translated into better ways to understand disease, identify meaningful biological signals, evaluate interventions and ultimately improve healthcare.

Emerging technologies — including advanced cell-based systems, molecular profiling, quantitative imaging, computational approaches and AI-assisted research tools — are especially interesting to me where they strengthen biological discovery and translational decision-making.

Beyond Experiments

Science as a community

Scientific work is also shaped by the people, conversations and communities surrounding it.

Alongside laboratory research, I have taken part in scientific representation, mentoring, interdisciplinary communication and community-building activities.

I co-founded Gene & Tonic at the Cité internationale universitaire de Paris as an informal space where researchers, students and scientifically curious communities can meet through talks, discussion and interdisciplinary exchange.

These activities reflect something I value increasingly in science: creating environments where scientific ideas can move beyond disciplinary boundaries and generate new conversations.
