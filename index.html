About

A scientific journey shaped by connection.

I am a cell and molecular biologist whose research has progressively connected cellular stress, ageing, molecular communication and functional biology.

My international research experience across Ireland, France and Germany has taken me from redox biology and cellular stress through endothelial senescence, extracellular-vesicle research, quantitative proteomics and mammalian cell-based functional assays.

Research

Cellular state, communication and function.

A central question connects much of my work: how does a change in cellular state alter communication with other cells, and what functional consequences follow?

I investigate this question through cellular ageing and senescence, extracellular-vesicle biology, quantitative molecular profiling and functional mammalian cell-based approaches.

Together, these areas form a continuous scientific thread from cellular stress and ageing to altered molecular communication, recipient-cell responses and translational possibilities.

Scientific Outputs

Research across interconnected questions.

My published work reflects the progression of my scientific interests from redox protein biology to extracellular-vesicle research and collaborative cardiovascular science.

These outputs span methodological work in redox proteomics, synthesis of extracellular- vesicle biology in atherosclerosis, and collaborative consensus work arising from the Fourth Maastricht Consensus Conference on Thrombosis.

My current first-author original research on age-associated endothelial extracellular-vesicle proteomics and function is under peer review (2026).

Science & Community

Science grows through conversation.

Beyond laboratory research, I contribute to scientific exchange, community building and accessible science communication.
