Scientific Journey

From cellular stress to intercellular communication.

My scientific trajectory has developed across redox and cellular-stress biology, therapeutic delivery research, endothelial ageing and senescence, extracellular-vesicle biology, quantitative proteomics and functional mammalian cell-based assays.

Scientific Evolution

Each stage of my research has added a new layer to a broader interest in how cellular state influences biological function and communication.

Early work in mitochondrial and redox biology introduced me to cellular stress and disease mechanisms. Translational research subsequently brought extracellular vesicles and biological delivery systems into my work.

My doctoral research then connected cellular stress with ageing and senescence in endothelial systems, while extracellular-vesicle biology and quantitative proteomics provided ways to investigate altered intercellular communication at both functional and molecular levels.

More recently, my postdoctoral research extended this progression towards quantitative mammalian cell-based assays for measuring functional intracellular delivery.

Research Experience

Scientific Journey

Research and training across academic, collaborative and industry-linked environments in France, Germany and Ireland.

Apr 2025 — Mar 2026

Postdoctoral Researcher

Lavieu Lab · Université Paris Cité · Paris, France

Worked with quantitative mammalian cell-based systems for evaluating functional cargo delivery.

I optimised and standardised quantitative NanoLuc-based functional assays in engineered HeLa donor–recipient systems to evaluate EV-mediated cargo delivery. The work included reproducible assay workflows and collaborative evaluation of modified extracellular vesicles.

This stage of my research strengthened my experience in quantitative assay optimisation, experimental controls, transfection-based systems, reproducibility and structured troubleshooting.

Jan 2020 — Dec 2023

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Doctoral Researcher

TiCardio Innovative Training Network · Aix-Marseille Université / C2VN · France & Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz / CTH · Germany

My doctoral research investigated how ageing and cellular senescence alter endothelial-cell biology and extracellular-vesicle-mediated intercellular communication.

The work combined primary human endothelial-cell models, prolonged cell culture, cellular senescence assessment, extracellular-vesicle isolation and characterisation, recipient-cell experiments, functional assays and quantitative proteomics.

Connecting the Work

Cellular stress → ageing and senescence → altered intercellular communication → molecular profiling → functional delivery.

Although the experimental systems have changed, the underlying questions have remained connected: how cells respond to altered states, how those changes are communicated, and how their biological consequences can be measured quantitatively.
