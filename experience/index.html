Scientific Journey

From cellular stress to quantitative functional biology.

My scientific development has progressed through research environments in Ireland, France and Germany, with each stage adding a new perspective on cellular state, molecular communication and biological function.

The Progression

Questions that evolved over time.

My career has not followed a series of isolated topics. Each research stage has expanded the biological question I was asking.

Early work on cellular stress and redox biology introduced me to molecular regulation within cells. Translational research then brought extracellular vesicles and biological delivery systems into that perspective.

Doctoral research connected those ideas with endothelial ageing, cellular senescence and intercellular communication, while later work extended the trajectory towards quantitative functional cell biology.

Research Experience

Scientific journey

A progression from fundamental cellular mechanisms toward quantitative and translational biology.

Foundations

University of Delhi · India

Life Sciences

Undergraduate training established my foundation across the biological sciences and led to a growing interest in cellular and molecular mechanisms of disease.

Postgraduate Training

University of Galway · Ireland

Neuropharmacology

Postgraduate research introduced me to experimental questions involving cellular stress, mitochondrial dysfunction and molecular responses associated with disease.

This period established the cellular-stress perspective that would continue to influence my later research.

2017–2018

University of Galway · Ireland

Redox & Cellular Stress Biology

Research with the McDonagh group developed my experience in redox biology, protein regulation and cellular responses to oxidative stress.

This work also contributed to my early experience in protein-biochemistry methods and later resulted in a published methods chapter in redox proteomics.

2019

OmniSpirant Therapeutics / University of Galway · Ireland

Translational Extracellular-Vesicle Research

Working in an industry-linked research environment introduced me to extracellular vesicles from the perspective of therapeutic delivery and translational biotechnology.

This experience expanded my interests from molecular mechanisms within cells towards how biological material can be transferred between cells and potentially applied in therapeutic contexts.

2020–2023

MSCA TiCardio · Aix-Marseille Université / Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz

Ageing, Senescence & Intercellular Communication

My doctoral research brought together endothelial biology, ageing, cellular senescence and extracellular-vesicle-mediated communication.

Conducted through an international cotutelle within the Marie Skłodowska-Curie TiCardio network, the PhD combined research environments in France and Germany and included additional scientific mobility and collaborative work.

This period established the central scientific question that continues to guide my work: how changes in cellular state influence molecular communication and downstream biological function.

Doctoral Secondment

Leibniz Institute for Analytical Sciences — ISAS · Dortmund

Quantitative Proteomics

A research secondment at ISAS expanded my doctoral work into quantitative proteomics and molecular profiling.

This experience strengthened my ability to connect experimental cellular biology with large molecular datasets and pathway-level biological interpretation.

2025–2026

Lavieu Lab · Université Paris Cité · Paris

Quantitative Functional Cell Biology

During a fixed-term postdoctoral appointment, I worked with quantitative mammalian cell-based systems for evaluating functional cargo delivery.

My work focused on optimisation and standardisation of reproducible reporter-based functional assays, quantitative analysis, experimental controls and structured troubleshooting.

This stage extended my previous interest in intercellular communication towards a more quantitative question: how can successful intracellular delivery be distinguished from a measurable functional biological effect?

The Scientific Thread

Each stage added another level.

Cellular stress led to molecular communication; molecular communication led to extracellular vesicles; ageing introduced altered cellular state; proteomics added molecular resolution; quantitative assays brought the focus to measurable function.

Together, these experiences shape how I now approach biological questions: by connecting mechanism, quantitative experimentation and functional consequence rather than treating individual techniques as isolated expertise.
