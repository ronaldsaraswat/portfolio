Scientific Journey

From cellular stress to quantitative functional biology.

My scientific development has progressed through research environments in Ireland, France and Germany, with each stage adding a new perspective on cellular state, molecular communication and biological function.

The Progression

One question leading to another.

My career has not developed through isolated research topics, but through a sequence of connected biological questions.

Early work in cellular stress and redox biology led towards translational extracellular-vesicle research. Doctoral training then brought ageing, senescence and intercellular communication into focus, while quantitative proteomics provided molecular resolution.

Postdoctoral work in Paris extended that trajectory into quantitative mammalian cell-based systems and functional biological readouts.

Scientific Journey

Training across disciplines and research environments.

Postgraduate Training

Neuropharmacology

University of Galway · Ireland

Postgraduate training expanded my interest in molecular mechanisms of disease and experimentally driven biomedical research.

2017–2018

Redox & Cellular Stress Biology

University of Galway · Ireland

Research experience in redox protein biology introduced me to how molecular stress responses alter cellular behaviour and protein function.

2019

Translational Extracellular-Vesicle Research

OmniSpirant Therapeutics / University of Galway

This stage introduced a translational dimension to extracellular-vesicle biology and strengthened my interest in how biological carriers can be investigated in therapeutic contexts.

2020–2023

Ageing, Senescence & Intercellular Communication

Marie Skłodowska-Curie TiCardio · Aix-Marseille Université / Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz

My doctoral research connected endothelial ageing and cellular senescence with extracellular-vesicle-mediated communication, molecular profiling and cardiovascular functional biology within an international cotutelle and doctoral-training environment.

Doctoral Secondment

Quantitative Proteomics

ISAS Dortmund · Germany

Proteomics training added molecular-scale resolution to my research, supporting the quantitative investigation and interpretation of extracellular-vesicle protein composition.

2025–2026

Quantitative Functional Cell Biology

Lavieu Lab · Université Paris Cité · France

During my fixed-term postdoctoral appointment, I worked with quantitative mammalian cell-based systems for evaluating functional cargo delivery. My work focused on optimisation and standardisation of quantitative reporter-based assays, reproducible experimental workflows, experimental controls, quantitative analysis and structured troubleshooting.

Scientific Thread

A connected trajectory.

Cellular stress led to molecular communication; molecular communication led to extracellular vesicles; ageing introduced altered cellular state; proteomics added molecular resolution; quantitative assays brought the focus to measurable function.

Together, these experiences have shaped a scientific perspective centred on connecting mechanism, quantitative experimentation and functional consequence rather than treating individual techniques as isolated expertise.
