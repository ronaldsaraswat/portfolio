Research

Cellular state, communication & function.

My research asks how changes in cellular state — particularly during stress, ageing and senescence — alter molecular communication between cells and influence biological function.

Central Question

What changes when the cell changes?

How do changes in cellular state alter molecular communication between cells, and how do these changes influence tissue function, ageing and disease?

This question has progressively connected different areas of my work: cellular stress, endothelial ageing and senescence, extracellular-vesicle biology, quantitative proteomics and functional cell-based research.

Rather than treating these as separate topics, I view them as different levels of the same biological problem — from changes within a cell to the signals it produces, the information communicated to neighbouring cells and the resulting functional response.

Cellular State → Communication → Function → Molecular Discovery
01

Cellular Ageing · Senescence · Endothelial Biology

Cellular State

How does ageing reshape cellular phenotype?

During my doctoral research, I investigated cellular ageing and senescence using primary human endothelial-cell systems.

The work examined how prolonged changes in cellular state were reflected in senescence, cellular-stress, cell-cycle and endothelial activation phenotypes, and how these changes could influence interactions with recipient cells.

Biological Context Endothelial ageing and cellular senescence
Experimental Level Primary human endothelial-cell systems
Scientific Focus Cellular-state change and downstream function
02

Extracellular Vesicles · Intercellular Communication

Communication

How is altered cellular state communicated to other cells?

Extracellular vesicles became a central part of my research because they connect changes occurring within a cell to potential effects beyond that cell.

My work has involved EV isolation and preparation, particle and biophysical characterisation, cytometric approaches, EV tracking and recipient-cell experiments designed to examine how vesicle-associated communication relates to biological function.

Scientific Principle

The biological state of a cell may influence not only its internal molecular profile, but also the information it communicates to neighbouring cells.

03

Quantitative Cell Biology · Functional Readouts

Functional Delivery

When does delivery become biological function?

During my fixed-term postdoctoral research in the Lavieu Lab at Université Paris Cité, I worked with quantitative mammalian cell-based systems for evaluating functional cargo delivery.

I optimised and standardised quantitative NanoLuc-based functional assays in engineered HeLa donor–recipient systems to evaluate EV-mediated cargo delivery.

This work centred on reproducible assay workflows, transfection optimisation, experimental controls, quantitative analysis and structured troubleshooting.

Collaborative work also involved evaluating cellular uptake and functional intracellular delivery using modified extracellular-vesicle delivery systems.

Central Focus

Quantitative assessment of whether intracellular delivery produces a measurable functional biological readout.

04

Proteomics · Molecular Profiling · Biological Interpretation

Molecular Discovery

What molecular changes accompany altered communication?

Quantitative proteomics provided a molecular layer through which I could investigate changes associated with endothelial-cell state and extracellular-vesicle biology.

During research involving ISAS Dortmund, I integrated quantitative proteomics with experimental studies of endothelial extracellular vesicles.

Sample Preparation S-Trap preparation and EV samples for LC-MS/MS proteomics
Quantitative Analysis MaxQuant · Perseus · R
Biological Interpretation Cytoscape · Reactome · FunRich
Analytical Goal

Move from large molecular datasets toward biologically interpretable pathways, candidate processes and experimentally meaningful questions.

Research Framework

One biological continuum.

The four programmes are connected by a common progression from cellular state to biological consequence.

01 · Cellular State

Stress, ageing and senescence alter cellular phenotype.

02 · Communication

Altered cells may change the molecular information communicated to other cells.

03 · Recipient Function

Communication becomes biologically meaningful when it changes recipient-cell behaviour or produces a functional readout.

04 · Molecular Discovery

Quantitative profiling helps identify molecular processes underlying these changes.
