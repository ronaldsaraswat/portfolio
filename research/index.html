Research

Cellular state, communication and function.

My research examines how cellular stress, ageing and disease alter molecular communication between cells and how these changes influence cellular and tissue function. I approach these questions through mammalian cell biology, extracellular-vesicle research, quantitative functional assays, molecular analysis, microscopy and proteomics.

Research Question

How do changes in cellular state alter molecular communication between cells, and how do these changes influence ageing, tissue function and disease?

My scientific trajectory has developed from redox biology and cellular stress through endothelial ageing and senescence, extracellular-vesicle biology, quantitative molecular profiling and functional delivery research.

These areas are connected by a common interest: understanding how altered cellular states are communicated to neighbouring cells and how those signals influence biological function.

I

Cellular State

Cellular Ageing, Senescence & Endothelial Biology

How does endothelial ageing change cellular phenotype and communication with neighbouring cells?

My doctoral research within the French-German Marie Skłodowska-Curie TiCardio programme investigated how ageing and cellular senescence alter endothelial-cell biology and intercellular communication.

I worked with primary human endothelial-cell models and replicative-senescence systems, combining prolonged cell culture, cellular phenotyping, stress and senescence assessment, endothelial activation studies and recipient-cell experiments.

Experimental systems

Primary human aortic endothelial cells, HUVEC models and prolonged cell-culture systems.

Cellular phenotyping

Senescence, DNA-damage, cell-cycle, cellular-stress and endothelial activation readouts.

Functional biology

Recipient-cell experiments and thrombo-inflammatory functional assessment.

II

Intercellular Communication

Extracellular Vesicles & Intercellular Communication

How does cellular state influence the information transported between cells through extracellular vesicles?

Extracellular vesicles became a central connection between my work on cellular ageing and my broader interest in intercellular communication.

During my doctoral research, I investigated ageing-associated changes in endothelial extracellular vesicles and evaluated their effects on naïve recipient cells and thrombo-inflammatory responses.

EV preparation

Differential centrifugation and size-exclusion chromatography.

Characterisation

Particle analysis, biophysical characterisation and flow-cytometric approaches.

Functional communication

EV tracking together with recipient-cell functional experiments.

III

Functional Delivery

EV-Mediated Functional Cargo Delivery

How can biological cargo delivery be evaluated through measurable intracellular function rather than cellular uptake alone?

During my fixed-term postdoctoral appointment at Université Paris Cité, I worked with quantitative mammalian cell-based systems designed to evaluate extracellular-vesicle-mediated functional cargo delivery.

My contribution focused on optimisation and standardisation of quantitative functional assays, experimental controls and reproducible workflows. I did not originate the underlying delivery technology.

Engineered EV delivery

I worked with engineered mammalian donor-recipient systems to assess extracellular-vesicle-mediated functional cargo delivery.

My work centred on establishing robust and reproducible cell-based workflows for quantitative assessment of functional intracellular delivery.

  • Mammalian cell-based assay optimisation
  • Reporter-based functional readouts
  • Transfection optimisation
  • Experimental controls
  • Quantitative analysis
  • Reproducibility and structured troubleshooting

Modified EV delivery systems

Through a cross-institute collaboration, I contributed to evaluating cellular uptake and functional intracellular delivery using modified extracellular-vesicle systems.

A central conceptual development in this work was the transition from evaluating vesicle presence or cellular uptake towards quantitative assessment of whether delivered cargo produces a functional intracellular readout.
IV

Molecular Discovery

Proteomics & Molecular Discovery

How can quantitative molecular profiling reveal biologically meaningful changes associated with ageing and extracellular-vesicle biology?

Proteomics represents the molecular-discovery component of my ageing and extracellular-vesicle research.

During my doctoral work and research secondment at ISAS Dortmund, I integrated quantitative proteomics with my experimental studies of age-associated endothelial extracellular vesicles.

This work combined experimental sample preparation, LC-MS/MS-derived quantitative datasets, differential-abundance analysis, pathway interpretation and biological prioritisation.

Experimental

S-Trap sample preparation and preparation of EV samples for LC-MS/MS proteomics.

Quantitative analysis

MaxQuant, Perseus, R and exploratory Bioconductor workflows.

Biological interpretation

Cytoscape, Reactome, FunRich, pathway-level interpretation and biological prioritisation.
