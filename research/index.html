Research

Cellular state, communication and function.

My research examines how cellular stress, ageing and disease alter molecular communication between cells and how these changes influence cellular and tissue function. I approach these questions using mammalian cell models, extracellular-vesicle biology, quantitative functional assays, molecular profiling, microscopy and proteomics.

Research Question

How do changes in cellular state alter molecular communication between cells, and how do these changes influence ageing, tissue function and disease?

My scientific trajectory has developed from redox biology and cellular stress through endothelial ageing and senescence, extracellular vesicles, quantitative proteomics and functional cargo-delivery systems.

These areas are connected by a common interest: understanding how altered cellular states are communicated to neighbouring cells and how those signals produce measurable biological effects.

I

Cellular State

Cellular Ageing, Senescence & Endothelial Biology

How does endothelial ageing change cellular phenotype and communication with neighbouring cells?

My doctoral research within the French-German Marie Skłodowska-Curie TiCardio programme examined how ageing and cellular senescence alter endothelial cells and their extracellular-vesicle-mediated communication.

I worked with primary human aortic endothelial cells and HUVEC-based replicative-senescence models, including prolonged cell culture, young and older donor comparisons, cellular stress and senescence assessment, endothelial activation and functional recipient-cell experiments.

Experimental systems

Primary HAoEC, HUVEC, long-term culture and replicative-senescence models.

Cellular phenotyping

SA-β-gal, γH2AX, cell-cycle and stress markers, endothelial activation and TNF-α-associated responses.

Functional biology

Recipient-cell experiments and thrombo-inflammatory functional readouts.

II

Intercellular Communication

Extracellular Vesicles & Intercellular Communication

How does cellular state influence the information transported between cells through extracellular vesicles?

Extracellular vesicles became a central link between my work on cellular ageing and my broader interest in intercellular communication.

During my doctoral research, I investigated ageing-associated changes in endothelial EVs and examined whether vesicles released by senescent endothelial cells could influence naïve recipient cells and thrombo-inflammatory responses.

EV preparation

Differential centrifugation and size-exclusion chromatography.

Characterisation

TRPS/qNano, NTA, DLS, zeta potential and flow-cytometric approaches.

Functional communication

EV uptake and tracking together with recipient-cell functional experiments.

III

Functional Delivery

EV-Mediated Functional Cargo Delivery

How can EV-associated cargo delivery be measured by functional intracellular effects rather than uptake alone?

During my fixed-term postdoctoral appointment in the Lavieu Lab at Université Paris Cité, I worked on quantitative mammalian cell-based systems for evaluating extracellular-vesicle-mediated functional cargo delivery.

My role focused on optimising and standardising the experimental and quantitative assay workflows; I did not originate the underlying delivery technology.

Engineered EV delivery

Engineered HeLa donor-recipient systems using artificial-fusogen overexpression were used to investigate enhanced EV fusion and functional cargo delivery.

  • Plasmid preparation
  • Lipofectamine optimisation
  • Donor and recipient experimental conditions
  • EV-dose optimisation
  • NanoLuc functional readouts
  • Experimental controls
  • Complementary cytotoxicity and functional assessment
  • Reproducibility and structured troubleshooting

Modified and lipid-hybridised EVs

In a cross-institute collaboration with IPNP, I contributed to evaluating cellular uptake and functional intracellular delivery of modified and lipid-hybridised extracellular vesicles.

The conceptual progression was from measuring whether extracellular vesicles are present or internalised towards determining whether their associated cargo produces a measurable functional intracellular effect.
IV

Molecular Discovery

Proteomics & Molecular Discovery

How can quantitative molecular profiling reveal biologically meaningful changes associated with ageing and extracellular-vesicle function?

Proteomics represents the molecular-discovery arm of my ageing and extracellular-vesicle research. During my doctoral collaboration and secondment at ISAS Dortmund, I contributed to quantitative proteomic analysis of age-associated endothelial extracellular vesicles.

This work combined experimental sample preparation, quantitative mass-spectrometry workflows, differential-abundance analysis, pathway interpretation and biological prioritisation.

Experimental

S-Trap sample preparation and LC-MS/MS-based proteomic workflows.

Quantitative analysis

MaxQuant, Perseus, R and exploratory Bioconductor workflows.

Biological interpretation

Cytoscape, Reactome, FunRich, pathway enrichment and candidate prioritisation.
